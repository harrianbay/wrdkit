Twitter / X character limits in 2026 — every limit explained

June 8, 2026 5 min read By WrdKit Team

Twitter rebranded to X in 2023 and expanded character limits for paying subscribers — but most people still don't know all the limits, how URLs are counted, or what the thread rules actually are. This guide covers every character limit on X in 2026.

How URLs are counted

This trips up almost everyone. Twitter has counted URLs differently from regular text since 2011. Every URL — regardless of its actual length — is automatically shortened to a t.co link and counted as exactly 23 characters.

This means:

  • A 5-character URL like x.com still costs you 23 characters.
  • A 200-character URL still costs you just 23 characters.
  • If you attach an image, video, or card to a tweet, the media attachment link also consumes 23 characters.

This is critical to understand when writing tweets with links — you always have 280 - 23 = 257 characters left for your actual text if you're including a link.

Thread limits

There is no official cap on how many tweets you can chain in a thread, but X limits the number of tweets you can post in a 24-hour period based on your account type. In practice, threads of 25+ tweets are rarely a problem for normal users.

Each tweet in a thread follows your account's standard character limit — 280 for free users, 4,000 for Premium. When composing a thread, the X interface lets you add new tweet slots with the "+" button before posting the whole thing at once.

What happens when you go over the limit?

The X compose box shows a countdown character counter that turns red as you approach the limit, then goes into negative numbers if you keep typing. You physically cannot tweet until you're at or under the character limit — the Tweet button stays greyed out.

Writing tweets that fit every time

The easiest way to draft tweets without hitting the limit is to write them in a character counter tool first, where you can see your count in real time without the pressure of the X interface.

Tips for staying under 280:

  • Cut filler words: "in order to" → "to", "at this point in time" → "now".
  • Use contractions: "do not" → "don't", "it is" → "it's".
  • Move your link to the end — X will convert it to t.co regardless.
  • If you need more space, start a thread rather than cramming everything into one tweet.
