How to go from 40 WPM to 80 WPM in 30 days

June 8, 2026 8 min read By WrdKit Team

Doubling your typing speed in a month sounds ambitious — but it's a realistic goal for most people, because the average untrained typist has major inefficiencies that are easy to fix. If you're at 40 WPM and still hunt-and-peck occasionally, 80 WPM is genuinely 30 days away. Here's how.

Why your current speed is probably artificially low

Most people who type at 40–55 WPM fall into one or more of these categories:

  • They look at the keyboard while typing (even occasionally).
  • They use the wrong fingers for certain keys.
  • They type in bursts rather than at a consistent rhythm.
  • They never formally learned touch typing — they developed their own system.

The good news: every one of these is fixable. The bad news: fixing them requires slowing down before you speed up. Expect your WPM to drop to 20–25 in week one before it climbs. That's normal and necessary.

Touch typing: the foundation

Touch typing means typing without looking at the keyboard, using the correct finger for every key. It's not optional — it's the prerequisite for anything above 70 WPM with accuracy.

Home row position

Your fingers should rest on the home row at all times when not actively striking a key: A S D F for the left hand, J K L ; for the right. The F and J keys have raised bumps so you can find home position without looking.

Every key on the keyboard has an assigned finger. Your left index finger covers F, G, R, T, V, B. Your right index finger covers J, H, Y, U, N, M. Both pinkies are responsible for their respective outer columns — the hardest keys to train.

The 5 technique fixes that actually move the needle

1. Stop looking at the keyboard — immediately

Put a blank cloth over your keyboard if you have to. Looking down destroys your rhythm, adds a half-second delay to every unfamiliar key, and prevents muscle memory from forming. The fix is painful for the first 3–5 days and then it clicks permanently.

2. Fix your wrist position

Your wrists should hover slightly above the keyboard, not rest on the desk. Resting wrists limit finger range and cause repetitive strain injuries over time. Your elbows should be at roughly 90 degrees with your forearms parallel to the floor.

3. Prioritize accuracy over speed

Speed without accuracy is useless — you'll just be fast at making mistakes. Always aim for 98%+ accuracy. If you're dropping below 95%, slow down. Speed follows naturally from accuracy once your muscle memory is solid.

4. Type in rhythm, not bursts

Most slow typists type in bursts — fast on easy words, slow on hard ones. Consistent rhythm (like a metronome) is what separates 60 WPM typists from 100 WPM typists. Practice drills at a speed where you can maintain consistent cadence across the entire sentence.

5. Train weak fingers deliberately

Your ring finger and pinky are almost certainly undertrained. They handle keys like Q, A, Z (left pinky), P, semicolon, slash (right pinky), and W, S, X (left ring). Do dedicated drills on these columns. 15 minutes of pinky-focused practice beats one hour of general typing practice.

Your 30-day plan: week by week

Week 1 — Days 1–7

Relearn the keyboard correctly (expect 20–30 WPM)

Spend 20 minutes daily on home row drills only. Do not advance until you can type ASDFGHJKL; rows at 40 WPM with 98% accuracy without looking. Cover your keyboard. Use Keybr, TypingClub, or WrdKit's typing speed test to drill specific keys. Ignore your WPM — it will drop. That's the plan.

Week 2 — Days 8–14

Expand to full keyboard rows (target: 35–45 WPM)

Add the top row (QWERTY) and bottom row (ZXCVBNM). Still no speed pressure. Practice common English words — "the", "and", "that", "have", "this" — because they account for 50% of everything you type. 20–25 minutes daily. By day 14 you should type common words without hesitation.

Week 3 — Days 15–21

Build rhythm and consistency (target: 50–65 WPM)

Now you introduce speed work. Do 1-minute timed tests at 80% of your max speed — the rhythm zone. Practice numbers and punctuation (they're often ignored and kill speed later). Type real sentences, not just word lists. 25 minutes daily. Track your scores every day.

Week 4 — Days 22–30

Push for 80 WPM — targeted sprints

Do 2-minute tests at maximum comfortable speed. Focus on your 5 slowest keys and drill them for 5 minutes before each session. Type from real writing — copy blog posts, news articles, your own drafts. Real text trains you for real work. By day 30, most committed practitioners hit 75–90 WPM.

Tracking your progress

Measure your WPM and accuracy at the same time every day — ideally at the start of your session before fatigue sets in. Log the numbers. Progress isn't linear: you'll plateau for 2–3 days, then jump 5–8 WPM. Seeing the trend is what keeps you motivated through plateaus.
