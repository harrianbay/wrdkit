The complete word count guide for 2026 — blog posts, essays, novels & more

June 8, 2026 9 min read By WrdKit Team

Word count is one of the most searched questions in writing — and one of the most misunderstood. There is no single "right" number. The ideal word count depends entirely on what you're writing, who you're writing for, and what you want to achieve. This guide breaks it down for every major content type.

Why word count matters (and when it doesn't)

Word count matters for three reasons: SEO rankings, reader expectations, and hard limits (college apps, social platforms, academic assignments).

For SEO, longer content tends to rank better for competitive keywords — not because Google rewards length, but because comprehensive content naturally earns more backlinks and covers more search intent. For reader expectations, a 300-word "how to start a business" post will feel thin. A 4,000-word grocery list post will feel exhausting. Matching length to intent is the skill.

Blog post word counts for SEO

This is the most searched question in content marketing, and the answer has become clearer over the past few years.

Post TypeRecommended Word CountWhy
Quick answer / FAQ300–600 wordsTargets featured snippets
Standard blog post1,000–1,500 wordsCovers the topic adequately
SEO-focused article1,500–2,500 wordsOutranks thin content on most keywords
Pillar / cornerstone page2,500–4,000 wordsTargets competitive head terms
In-depth guide / resource4,000–8,000 wordsEarns links, ranks for clusters
Key insight: The average first-page Google result contains around 1,400–1,500 words for most keyword types. For competitive keywords ("best CRM software", "how to lose weight"), top-ranking pages average 2,000–2,500 words. Match or exceed competitor length, then beat them on quality.

College and academic essay word counts

Academic writing has strict limits you must follow exactly. Going over the limit — even by a few words — signals you can't follow instructions. Going significantly under signals you haven't fully addressed the prompt.

Undergraduate paper1,500–5,000 words

PhD dissertation70,000–100,000 words

The Common App 650-word rule

The Common App enforces a hard 650-word maximum for the personal statement. You cannot submit if you exceed it. Most admissions officers recommend targeting 620–650 words — close to the limit, but not padding to fill it. Use our free word counter to track this precisely as you draft.

Email word counts

Email has its own set of best practices depending on the type of email you're sending.

  • Cold outreach email: 50–125 words. Short, punchy, one clear ask.
  • Newsletter: 200–500 words for curated newsletters; up to 800 for long-form editorial newsletters.
  • Sales email: 75–150 words. People don't read long sales emails.
  • Professional / workplace email: 50–200 words. Get to the point.
  • Email marketing campaign: 200–300 words with a strong CTA.

How to count words accurately

Different tools count words differently. Microsoft Word, Google Docs, and WrdKit's word counter may give slightly different numbers because they handle hyphenated words, contractions, and numbers differently. For academic work with strict limits, always use the same tool throughout your writing process and double-check before submitting.

Does word count include footnotes and references?

This varies by institution. Most UK universities exclude footnotes and bibliography. Most US universities include everything in the body but exclude the works cited page. Always check your assignment rubric — and when in doubt, ask your instructor before you submit.

The bottom line on word count

Word count is a proxy, not a goal. Write as many words as the topic needs to be fully covered — no more, no less. For SEO content, that's usually 1,500–2,500 words for most topics. For college essays, respect the limit precisely. For novels, know what agents in your genre expect before you query.
